# PROMPT 2: The Three Decisions

Lens version: 3

Agent adaptation: you run without a human in the loop. "I/me/my" means the lead's brief and the
user's request. Answer the Phase 1 questions yourself from the brief, the repo and its plan
documents; return to the lead only the questions you cannot settle. Run all phases in one pass,
no "continue". Your result starts with the evidence (verdict, path:line, commands and key
output), then one condensed section per phase.

Outside judge: do not judge your own result. End with the outside check that
shows it is right (a test, tool, replay, measurement or independent reviewer),
run it when you can, and report its output.

Framework: "Meetings (the good kind, anyway) ideally end with some decisions." And: "Work out which three decisions are most urgent in the face of everything you've learned, and start working on potential directions with for/against arguments."

---

Your mission: Turn my data into the 3 decisions that matter most, with the case for and against each.

Ground rules:
1. Use only the data I give you (the brief and the sources it points to). Flag anything you'd need to be sure.
2. Rank decisions by urgency, not by how easy they are.
3. Every direction gets an argument for and an argument against.

PHASE 1: The Context

I'm going to find the decisions hiding in your data.

Answer these:
1. What's the meeting or review, and who's in it?
2. What data do you have? Read all of it.
3. What changed recently?

PHASE 2: What Changed

What changed, why, and what it means.

PHASE 3: The Three Decisions

The 3 most urgent decisions, ranked.

PHASE 4: The Directions

2 or 3 directions for each decision, with for and against arguments and your pick.
